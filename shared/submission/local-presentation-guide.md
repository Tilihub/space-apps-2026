# Local Event presentation guide

The presentation guide from our Local Event's organisers (Greek NewSpace Society, November 2026):
[local-presentation-guide.pdf](local-presentation-guide.pdf), 7 slides.
**Read this before deciding anything about the presentation:** who speaks, the slides, the demo,
the timing.

The summary below is in our own words, as text, so every AI tool can read it. If the summary and
the PDF disagree, the PDF wins.

## Event information (PDF slide 2)

- **Time:** 7 minutes to present, then a short Q&A with the judges. Time management matters.
- **Slide format:** PDF is recommended, to avoid font and layout problems on another computer.
  PowerPoint or web slides (Canva, Google Slides) also work.
- **Language:** slides ideally in English, because the final project goes to the global Space Apps
  platform. The spoken presentation can be in Greek (most teams) or English.
- **Live demo:** encouraged if the prototype works, but have screenshots or videos ready as a
  backup. The slide also says the app must be deployed and publicly available.

## How the judges score (PDF slide 3)

1. **Impact:** how big the problem is, and how many people it could help.
2. **Creativity:** something new, or an improvement on what already exists.
3. **Validity:** scientifically sound; does what it claims; could work in the real world.
4. **Relevance:** answers the challenge we chose; how complete, feasible and usable it is.
5. **Presentation:** how well we tell the story (the challenge, the solution, why it matters),
   on the project page in particular.

## Recommended contents (PDF slide 5, optional)

The organisers say slides 4 to 6 are suggestions: we can follow, change or ignore them.

1. **The challenge:** which one, and the specific problem we tackle.
2. **Our solution:** the core idea, how it answers the challenge, why this approach.
3. **Space agency data and tech stack:** the NASA or other agency data we used, and our tools.
4. **Prototype or demo:** show it rather than describe it.
5. **Impact and viability:** who it's for, how it helps, what comes next.
6. **Conclusion:** end on a strong closing line.

## Tips (PDF slide 6, optional)

- Tell a story: a hook, the pain point, then our solution as the answer.
- Slides support what we say rather than replace it: images and diagrams, few words.
- Balance the time: not 5 minutes on the problem and 2 on the solution.
- Think of our weak points and prepare answers before the Q&A.

  global limit for the project page's demo link. One English PDF deck could serve both.
