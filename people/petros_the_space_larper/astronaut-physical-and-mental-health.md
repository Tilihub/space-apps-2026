# Physical and mental health during space missions

Research checked 2026-11-14. These priorities are proposals for the prototype. NASA's [Spaceflight Standard Measures](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-025-00532-6.pdf) describe what NASA collects and when. The [in-flight data inventory](astronaut-inflight-data.md) documents flight instruments and public data access.

## Mental health evidence from flight

| Source | What was observed | What the app can use |
|---|---|---|
| Jones et al. (2022), 24 ISS astronauts on roughly six-month missions | Astronauts reported about 6.5 hours of sleep per night in flight. Sleep under six hours was associated with slower psychomotor responses and higher reported stress and workload. The study paired repeated self-reports with a brief Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT-B). [Paper and figures](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsac006) | Compare sleep duration and timing, fatigue and sleepiness, perceived workload and stress on the same mission timeline. The study's sleep groups describe associations and provide no clinical alert threshold. |
| Barger et al. (2014), 64 Space Shuttle and 21 ISS participants | Wrist actigraphy estimated an average of 6.09 hours of sleep on ISS missions in this cohort. The study also collected sleep diaries. [Paper and supporting tables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4188436/) | Keep actigraphy estimates and the astronaut's report of sleep quality as separate observations. Collect both where available. |
| Stuster (2010), personal journals kept in orbit by ten ISS astronauts | Work, communication with people on Earth, adjustment, and interactions within the crew were prominent in the entries. Entries included positive and difficult experiences. Journal themes reflect the writers' experiences and the study's coding method. [NASA technical report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100026549/downloads/20100026549.pdf) | Allow crew members to report workload, connection to family and team, access to support, and what helped that day. Let them choose when to share. |
| Dev et al. (2024), 25 astronauts on approximately six-month ISS missions | There was no systematic decline in overall cognitive performance. Differences appeared in particular tests and phases, including sustained attention, processing speed and visual working memory. [Paper and mission-phase results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11614644/) | An optional standard PVT-B-style assessment can give a separate performance trend. Use the same task, device, and time of day for comparisons. Compare with preflight familiarization and baseline. |
| Inspiration4 (2024 publication), four participants on a three-day orbital flight | In-flight reports included moderate stress and high workload. Group findings did not show a clear preflight-to-in-flight change in mood or alertness. The sample and flight were small. [Nature paper, Fig. 5 and Supplementary Table 7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07648-x) | A report of high workload can coexist with stable mood. Capture these items individually. Public material includes study summaries, and access to individual source data depends on the repository. |
| Artemis II, NASA ARCHeR (2026) | NASA confirmed in-flight wrist recordings of activity and sleep. The study also examines crew and individual performance. NASA's task report describes analysis in progress after the mission. [Postflight report](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-2/nasas-artemis-ii-moon-mission-research-continues-on-earth/) · [ARCHeR task report](https://taskbook.nasaprs.com/tbp/index.cfm?action=taskbook_content&uid=CDBA0A4B91BA5173B3BBE7F0D22FAE2F) | Deep-space evidence is still developing. Keep mission and phase attached to results. NASA describes research access to processed, anonymized data through LSDA by request. |

NASA's ISS Standard Measures collect a morning sleep report and end-of-day reports on mood, team cohesion and performance, and habitability. NASA also requires psychological and behavioral support during the mission and private medical communications. The 2025 behavioral health evidence report discusses isolation, confinement, individual variability and possible underreporting. NASA's APEX conference abstract reports combined results from ISS and ground-analog participants. Its reported findings cover the combined sample. [NASA Standard Measures overview](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/spaceflight-standard-measures-characterizing-how-humans-adapt-in-space/) · [NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1, §6.2](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/) · [NASA 2025 evidence report](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/evidence/reports/HRP-ER-BMED-HFBP-20250210.pdf) · [APEX conference abstract](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250011721)

## Mental health fields for the prototype

| Timing | Proposed fields | Capture and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Morning check-in | Estimated sleep duration (hours, previous 24 h), bedtime and wake time, self-rated sleep quality, sleepiness | Use a wrist actigraph and/or an entered sleep diary. Keep the method visible. Record wake times in the mission time zone. Distinguish daytime sleepiness from mental or physical fatigue when both are collected. |
| End-of-day check-in | Mood, perceived stress, mental fatigue, perceived workload | Use stable anchors and the same recall period for each item. The [existing monitoring prototype](../angelos/health_monitor/README.md) already proposes `mood`, `stress` and `fatigue` on a 0–10 scale. Keep their directions explicit. These are product inputs for observing change over time. |
| Optional weekly or as needed | Connection with the crew or family, perceived support, effect on daily duties, and an option to request a private conversation with an authorized support person | Use short self-report prompts. The crew member chooses what to share and with whom. An unchecked item or a missed check-in is missing data. A request for support can be recorded independently of all scores. |
| Optional fixed interval | WHO-5 self-report of well-being, or a separate brief vigilance task | WHO-5 covers the previous two weeks. Keep its original items, response options and recall period if adopted as a complementary terrestrial instrument. No astronaut-specific cutoff was established by the sources reviewed. Record reaction time in ms and document the test protocol if using a vigilance task. [WHO-5](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/WHO-UCN-MSD-MHE-2024.01) · [ISS PVT-B protocol](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsac006) |

Show personal trends against a preflight reference collected with the same method. Pair changes with context the astronaut can add, such as a shifted schedule, a spacewalk, illness, or unusually heavy work. The app can summarize observations and let the astronaut explicitly request a private check-in. NASA schedules private psychological conferences and treats those communications as private. Keep personal answers private by default and use synthetic responses in the public demo. Heart rate and heart-rate variability can provide physiological context. A mental health diagnosis requires clinical assessment. No public individual mood series was verified for importing into the prototype. [NASA behavioral health evidence report](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/evidence/reports/HRP-ER-BMED-HFBP-20250210.pdf) · [NASA behavioral health support standard](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/)

## Physical indicators beyond the five vital signs

| Priority | Indicator | Capture and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| First release | Exercise and functional trend | Record type, duration (min), workload (W for cycling, load and repetitions for resistance work), and perceived exertion with its scale. Compare sessions at a similar workload. Add periodic aerobic capacity (VO₂peak) or measured strength only when a standardized test is available. A 46-astronaut ISS study analyzed in-flight exercise records and changes in fitness, strength and other systems. [ISS exercise study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9898566/) · [NASA exercise equipment](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/astronaut-exercise/) |
| First release | Physical symptoms and function | Log nausea or vomiting, dizziness or disorientation, headache, new vision changes, and impact on ordinary duties. Store onset, severity and mission phase. Shuttle crews reported in-flight motion sickness in standardized postflight interviews. Modern ISS ocular monitoring includes a symptom questionnaire, visual acuity and OCT. [Davis et al., Shuttle cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3240221/) · [ISS SANS study, Methods](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2839508) |
| First release | Fluid intake, with urine measurements when available | Record logged fluid intake in mL/day. Add measured 24-hour urine volume separately where mission protocols collect it. A study of 66 NASA astronauts reports urinary chemistry and volume before, during and after flight. Fluid intake provides context for interpreting urinary measures. [Astronaut renal study, Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49212-1) |
| Instrument or clinical integration | Eye and vascular exams | Store visual acuity by eye and OCT measurements with units and method when provided. Store jugular Doppler results as exam records with side and interpretation from the clinician. On-orbit ultrasound identified stagnant or reversed flow in six of 11 ISS crew members and an occlusive clot in one. [ISS ocular protocol](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2839508) · [ISS jugular ultrasound study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2755307) |
| Instrument or clinical integration | Blood and immune markers | Store hemoglobin, hematocrit, and selected iron markers when a lab result exists, with units and sample time. In an ISS study of 14 astronauts, breath carbon monoxide and serum iron markers provided evidence of increased hemolysis in flight. Keep each assay's own collection phase. [Trudel et al., Fig. 1 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01637-7) |
| Instrument or clinical integration | Personal radiation exposure | Store cumulative dose and dose rate when a personal dosimeter is available. Carry the quantity, unit, time interval, instrument and wear location. Spacecraft area readings can provide mission context. [NASA personal dosimetry](https://srag.jsc.nasa.gov/spaceradiation/how/how.cfm) · [In-flight data inventory](astronaut-inflight-data.md#other-verified-downloads-and-access-limits) |

## Countermeasures during missions

Operational measures are part of current mission practice or guidance. Flight studies report what happened under a particular protocol. Ground experiments and simulations inform future measures. The app actions below are proposed for the prototype and depend on the mission team's procedures.

### Mental health, sleep and crew performance

| Issue | Countermeasure and evidence | What the app could support |
|---|---|---|
| Distress, low mood or anxiety | NASA-STD-3001 requires a behavioral health support program and routinely scheduled private psychological conferences, with schedule set by the provider. For delayed links, NASA describes store-and-forward communication. ISS measures also include private family conferences, ground support for families and access to recreational resources. [NASA-STD-3001, §§6.2–6.3](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/) · [NASA behavioral health risk narrative](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/behavioral-health-risk-dag-narrative-approved-sa-06808.pdf) | Provide a crew-initiated private conversation with the authorized onboard or ground support role, according to mission procedures. Allow the astronaut to select details to share. If simulating a delayed link, show whether the request is queued, sent, or acknowledged. Keep recurring support available independently of survey scores. |
| Isolation and strained crew relationships | Set aside personal time and access to family contact and recreation. NASA also identifies crew and ground communication training and teamwork preparation. A five-campaign HERA/SIRIUS study examined perceived social support and stress in ground analogs. The Mars transit benefit and best timing of any individual measure remain uncertain. [NASA behavioral health report](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/evidence/reports/HRP-ER-BMED-HFBP-20250210.pdf) · [NASA team evidence report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220007465/downloads/Evidence%20Report%20-%20Team%202022%20FINAL4PUB%20rev%201.docx.pdf) · [HERA and SIRIUS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11781228/) | Show planned contact and protected personal time. Let crew request a private debrief, and model asynchronous family messages when the scenario uses a delayed link. |
| Sleep loss and circadian misalignment | NASA protects planned sleep periods, schedules shifts, and uses adjustable station lighting and sleep quarters that reduce noise and light. A 45-day HERA ground study found better circadian alignment with dynamic lighting. The observational ISS study connected short sleep with worse vigilance and higher stress. [NASA sleep measures](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/seven-ways-astronauts-improve-sleep-may-help-you-snooze-better-on-earth/) · [HERA lighting experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35996978/) · [ISS sleep and performance study](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsac006) | Put planned sleep and observed sleep on the same timeline. Tag schedule shifts and reported disruption from noise or light. Give the astronaut a way to ask about an adjustment to the next duty period. |
| Workload, fatigue and attention | NASA requires limits on work and planned sleep and describes task workload tools such as NASA-TLX. The brief vigilance test used on the ISS measured response speed and attention. [NASA-STD-3001, §4.3](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-0-human-performance/) · [ISS vigilance study](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsac006) | Show reported workload, fatigue and sleep alongside the duty schedule. Make the record available for a crew-led review of task timing, breaks or redistribution. Include reaction-time data only with the test protocol and baseline. |
| Self-guided coping with limited connectivity | Virtual Space Station modules for stress, conflict management and problem solving were evaluated for feasibility and acceptability in an eight-month HI-SEAS analog with six participants. Participants used focused breathing and guided muscle relaxation. ESA's VR Mental Care investigation used immersive sessions and questionnaires aboard the ISS. Evidence here supports feasibility or testing in the stated setting. [Anderson et al., HI-SEAS](https://drpst.dartmouth.edu/user_resources/publications/pdf/Anderson%20AMHP%202016-1.pdf) · [NASA ISS VR session log](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/stationreport/2023/12/06/iss-daily-summary-report-12-06-2023/) · [ESA flight demonstration](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Videos/2024/03/Virtual_Reality_for_body_and_mind) | Offer optional, offline coping materials reviewed by the mission's behavioral health team, with access to human support from the same screen. The app's prompt and activity history can remain private by default. |

### Physical health and environmental exposure

| Issue | Countermeasure and evidence | What the app could support |
|---|---|---|
| Aerobic, muscle and bone deconditioning | ISS crews use ARED resistance training, the T2 treadmill and CEVIS cycling. Losses in some outcomes persisted with current programs. The NASA Sprint flight study found broadly similar results on many outcomes with a shorter, more intense regimen in a small comparison. A separate small ISS study found better preservation of bone outcomes when clinician-managed alendronate was combined with ARED. [46-astronaut ISS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9898566/) · [Sprint flight study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7434884/) · [Bone study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23334732/) | Track the prescribed plan, actual load, effort, missed sessions, and comparable fitness tests. Record any adjustment agreed by the exercise specialist. |
| Space motion sickness, dizziness and reduced function | NASA describes preflight and inflight adaptation training, postlanding rehabilitation and operator-led medical management. Promethazine has flown for acute motion sickness, with selection and monitoring handled by the medical team because medication effects matter for duties. [NASA sensorimotor risk](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Risks/risk.aspx?i=88) · [NASA flight treatment study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19940007078) | Log symptom onset, vomiting, functional impact, mission phase, and the response recorded by the medical officer. |
| Orthostatic intolerance after landing | Crew use in-flight exercise and pre-landing fluid loading. A 2025 comparison found NASA's gradient compression garment mitigated cardiovascular stress during standing on landing day. Effects differed among garment designs. [Landing-day crew study](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00646.2024) | When simulating landing, show the mission's fluid-loading and garment checklist and record symptoms and supervised measurements by phase. |
| Vision changes and spaceflight-associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS) | In-flight visual checks and optical coherence tomography track changes. ISS crews carry corrective lenses for refractive shifts. In an on-orbit study, brief lower-body negative pressure did not reverse optic nerve head or retinal changes. Clinical prevention of structural SANS changes remains unresolved. [2026 expert consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13462731/) · [In-flight lower-body negative pressure study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2793385) | Record symptoms and exam results by eye, register changes in corrective lenses, and support follow-up with flight medicine. |
| Fluid intake and renal stone risk | NASA recommends access to water and adequate hydration. A placebo-controlled flight study found improved urinary stone-risk markers with potassium citrate. Clinical use is a flight-medicine decision. Urinary chemistry and measured 24-hour volume provide more direct markers than a drink log. [NASA urinary health brief](https://www.nasa.gov/ochmo-mtb-003-urinary-health-2/) · [Whitson et al., flight trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19765769/) | Show a mission-approved hydration plan, logged intake and separate results from urine collection, if available. Use a clinician-entered plan for any treatment. |
| Jugular flow changes and venous thrombosis | NASA describes duplex ultrasound screening during long-duration flights, remote guidance and risk-based clinical decisions. A published case documents physician-directed management in flight. NASA's 2026 working group describes a risk algorithm and limitations of estimating slow flow in orbit. [NASA clinical brief](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ochmo-mtb-007-venous-thrombosis-in-spaceflight.pdf) · [NASA 2026 risk algorithm](https://www.nasa.gov/organizations/ochmo/establishing-a-vte-risk-score-for-astronauts-algorithm/) · [Flight case](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc1905875) | Store exam dates, findings and clinical follow-up. A wearable cannot supply jugular Doppler results. |
| Hemolysis and nutritional status | An ISS study of 14 astronauts documented increased hemolysis in flight. Separate ISS observations found increases in iron stores during parts of flight. These findings support reviewing blood and iron measures with flight medicine and nutrition specialists. A proven intervention for microgravity-related hemolysis was not identified in the sources reviewed. A separate enhanced-diet intervention evaluated nutritional, immune and cognitive outcomes in a ground analog. [Trudel et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35031790/) · [ISS iron status study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23719548/) · [Ground-analog diet study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9755260/) | Display hemoglobin and iron results with sample date, units and phase. Keep fatigue as a separate report. Let the clinician document any nutrition or treatment plan. |
| Radiation exposure | NASA's operational measures combine crew dosimeters, cabin monitors, space-weather forecasts, shelter procedures for solar particle events and changes to EVA plans. The AstroRad Artemis I study used instrumented phantoms and modeled a severe solar event. Its predicted shielding benefit is specific to the modeled event and setup. [NASA radiation operations](https://srag.jsc.nasa.gov/spaceradiation/how/how.cfm) · [NASA Artemis II shelter procedures](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-2/to-protect-artemis-ii-astronauts-nasa-experts-keep-eyes-on-sun/) · [AstroRad primary study](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz1892) · [ESA explanation of measurements and modeling](https://blogs.esa.int/orion/2026/08/28/artemis-i-radiation-results-to-protect-astronauts/) | Show dose with units, sensor, location and time window. Display mission-authorized actions and acknowledgments alongside exposure data. |
| Heat strain and cabin air | EVA suits circulate cool water near the body. Cabin CO₂ is managed with ventilation and removal equipment. A study related ISS CO₂ readings to crew-reported headache at the population level. [NASA suit cooling](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/johnson/spacewalk-spacesuit-basics/) · [NASA cabin CO₂ brief](https://www.nasa.gov/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide-2/) · [ISS headache analysis](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140003857) | Put symptom timing alongside location-specific environmental readings and show the procedure in use. Keep environmental readings and individual vital signs as separate observations. |

For the Mars-transit demo, one example is a crew member reporting several nights of disrupted sleep, higher workload and feeling isolated. Show the relevant changes with their dates, offer a private request for support, and let a crew member record an agreed schedule adjustment and follow-up. If the simulated link is delayed, show the request state and available onboard contact. This is an example of supporting a crew decision under the proposed workflow.
