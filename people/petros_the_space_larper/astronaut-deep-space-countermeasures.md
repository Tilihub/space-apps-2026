# Astronaut health countermeasures for deep-space missions

Evidence checked 2026-11-14. Scope covers crewed lunar flight and proposed long-duration missions to Mars, especially transit. [General physical and mental health notes](astronaut-physical-and-mental-health.md) provide background for the existing prototype. The proposals below apply to the Mars-transit scenario and remain design options for the team.

## What has been tested

| Setting | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| **Crewed lunar flight** | The Apollo lunar mission records describe sleep disruption from operational schedules and cabin conditions. Four people flew on the nine-day Artemis II lunar flyby. NASA confirms wrist recordings in space for ARCHeR and use of Orion's compact exercise flywheel. Orion also passed through a planned roughly 40-minute communications blackout behind the Moon. The September 2026 ARCHeR update reports ongoing analysis. [Apollo medical operations review](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/tm-20205008196.pdf) · [Artemis II mission](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-ii/) · [Crew systems](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/crew-systems/) · [Lunar flyby log](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/04/06/artemis-ii-flight-day-6-lunar-flyby-updates/) · [ARCHeR task report](https://taskbook.nasaprs.com/tbp/index.cfm?action=taskbook_content&uid=CDBA0A4B91BA5173B3BBE7F0D22FAE2F) |
| **Uncrewed deep-space measurements** | Artemis I measured the Orion radiation environment and dose in instrumented phantoms. Curiosity's RAD measured radiation inside its spacecraft during cruise to Mars and on the surface. These measurements are suitable for instrument-specific environmental replay. [Artemis I study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07927-7) · [Mars cruise](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23723233/) · [Mars surface](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013JE004547) |
| **Mars simulations on Earth** | Mars500 confined six people for 520 days in a transit and surface mission simulation. NASA's second CHAPEA mission confined four volunteers for 378 days in a Mars surface habitat simulation, with one-way delays of up to 22 minutes. These studies inform human factors under isolation, workload and communication constraints. [Mars500 methods](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0093298&type=printable) · [CHAPEA Mission 2](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/johnson/chapea-mission-2-crew-reflects-after-378-days-on-simulated-mars/) |
| **Proposed crewed Mars missions** | There is no human Mars flight cohort. NASA reference studies describe one-way delays of roughly 4–24 minutes, depending on geometry and trajectory. Solar conjunction can disrupt communications, while emergency return may take months and routine resupply is unavailable in NASA reference scenarios. These are mission design assumptions. [NASA communications analysis, §8.5.2.1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220002905/downloads/NESC-RP-20-01589_NASA-TM-20220002905final.pdf) · [NASA Mars architecture challenges](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/10-2024-architecture-workshop-white-paper-mars-challenges.pdf) · [Earth-independent medical operations](https://doi.org/10.1177/10806032241310386) |

## Mental health and crew performance

| Problem | Deep-space evidence and proposed countermeasure | Prototype support |
|---|---|---|
| Distress when Earth is unavailable | NASA's Earth Independent Medical Operations concept places time-sensitive care with a trained crew medical officer (CMO) using onboard medical resources. NASA's medical standard supports scheduled private psychological communication and store-and-forward messaging when links are delayed. The Mars implementation is a planning concept. [NASA medical operations](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/) · [Exploration medical operations study](https://doi.org/10.1177/10806032241310386) | Provide access to the designated onboard support role and a separate private message to the designated Earth specialist. The astronaut selects details to share. Display queued, transmitted and acknowledged as distinct states. Use the onboard pathway during a simulated outage. |
| Isolation, monotony and conflict | The Mars500 study found strong individual differences. Most of the aggregate increase in depressive symptoms and distress came from one participant. Conflicts with mission control were reported more often than conflicts within the crew. The authors propose joint preflight training with controllers and greater onboard autonomy as possible responses. CHAPEA supplies an operational analog for delayed contact with Earth. [Mars500 behavioral study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0093298&type=printable) · [NASA CHAPEA Mission 2](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/johnson/chapea-mission-2-crew-reflects-after-378-days-on-simulated-mars/) | Use individual trends for mood, perceived workload, support and ability to carry out duties. Support private debriefs, protected personal time and asynchronous family contact. Let users control what is shared in debriefs. |
| Sleep, circadian drift and attention | Apollo crews reported schedule and comfort-related sleep loss. Artemis II scheduled sleep and recorded wrist activity. Mars500 observed falling activity and substantial differences in sleep timing. A dynamic-lighting study in a 45-day HERA ground simulation found improved circadian alignment. These results support protecting sleep blocks, timed lighting and review of task scheduling around fatigue. [Apollo review](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/tm-20205008196.pdf) · [Artemis II postflight report](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-2/nasas-artemis-ii-moon-mission-research-continues-on-earth/) · [Mars500 actigraphy](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1212646110) · [HERA lighting study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35996978/) | Show planned and observed sleep, actigraphy method, self-rated fatigue and duty periods in mission time. Record a crew-approved scheduling adjustment and review it at the next check-in. A brief reaction-time task can add a separate performance trend. |
| Coping while disconnected | Virtual Space Station modules for conflict, stress, and problem solving were tried by six participants in an eight-month HI-SEAS Mars analog. Focused breathing and guided muscle relaxation were used. This is feasibility and acceptability evidence for an autonomous resource. [Anderson et al., HI-SEAS](https://drpst.dartmouth.edu/user_resources/publications/pdf/Anderson%20AMHP%202016-1.pdf) | Make optional materials reviewed by the behavioral health team available offline. Keep journal access separate from case reporting, and keep the option to reach a person visible. |

## Physical health and environmental risks

| Problem | Deep-space evidence and proposed countermeasure | Prototype support |
|---|---|---|
| Loss of strength, bone and aerobic capacity | Orion's flywheel supported resistance and aerobic sessions in Artemis II, with about 30 minutes of exercise per person per day. The flight establishes use during a short mission. Long-duration hardware and prescriptions are still being developed. In the AGBRESA bed-rest trial, brief daily artificial gravity mitigated some losses in muscle function but did not preserve aerobic capacity. [Orion crew systems](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/crew-systems/) · [NASA exercise development](https://www.nasa.gov/glenn/glenn-expertise-space-exploration/human-health-performance/advanced-exercise-concepts/) · [AGBRESA trial](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-021-04673-w) | Compare prescribed and completed exercise, workload, effort and available equipment. Add mission-specific functional assessments and trends. Treat the Orion session duration as a flight observation. |
| Motion sickness, balance and planetary arrival | Apollo documentation records nausea and adaptation during lunar flights. NASA identifies gravity transitions and independent landing and egress as exploration risks. Train and rehearse priority tasks in relevant simulations and plan medical assessment and assistance around arrival. Evidence for physiological adaptation after months of Mars transit and entry into partial gravity is lacking. [Biomedical Results of Apollo](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19760005595/downloads/19760005595.pdf) · [NASA gravity hazards](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-gravity-fields/) · [Exploration mission challenges](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/10-2024-architecture-workshop-white-paper-mars-challenges.pdf) | Tag symptoms and functional impact by transit, arrival and surface phase. Show the mission's landing and recovery checklist. Keep medical actions under CMO review. |
| Vision changes, venous thrombosis and hemolysis | NASA's exploration medical concept prioritizes a trained CMO, point-of-care tools, local procedures and delayed specialist review. Deep-space efficacy for prevention of structural SANS, venous thrombosis or microgravity-related hemolysis remains unestablished in the reviewed human literature. Equipment selection and prevention or treatment protocols are mission medical decisions. [Exploration medical operations](https://doi.org/10.1177/10806032241310386) · [NASA exploration risk process](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/njp-microgravity-updates-to-the-nasa-human-system-risk-management-process-for-space-exploration.pdf) | Record visual symptoms, eye-specific results, clinician-interpreted ultrasound and laboratory values when available, with methods, units, times and a record for later specialist review. |
| Hydration, food and medical inventory | NASA's Mars scenarios require food and medical supplies to last through long storage and restricted resupply. NASA food experiments found that selected fortified low-moisture products retained vitamins over three years under specified ground storage conditions. NASA's drug stability strategy identifies formulation, packaging, storage and time as concerns for exploration. Reserve potable water and use the mission-approved hydration and nutrition plan. [Fortified food storage study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30059142/) · [NASA drug stability strategy](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001489/downloads/ExMC%20Strat%20for%20Drug%20Stability%20-%20May%202024%20final%201.pdf) · [NASA nutrition risk record](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/food-risk-record-rev-d-web-final.pdf) | Log fluid and food intake and any ordered urine measurements. Track lots, inventory, expiries and storage conditions using mission records. Let the authorized clinician or pharmacist review availability and substitutions. |
| Solar particle events and chronic galactic cosmic rays (GCR) | Orion's plan uses personal and cabin dosimetry, forecasts and a shielded location for solar events. Artemis I measurements showed that location and orientation affected exposure. The AstroRad paper used Artemis I measurements and historical storm modeling to estimate vest protection for a hypothetical severe solar event. For chronic GCR, exposure assessment depends on trajectory, duration, solar conditions and shielding. [Artemis II protection](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-2/to-protect-artemis-ii-astronauts-nasa-experts-keep-eyes-on-sun/) · [Artemis I radiation study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07927-7) · [AstroRad](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz1892) · [Mars cruise measurements](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23723233/) | Distinguish personal dosimeters, cabin instruments and robotic replay data. Display the measurement quantity, unit, position and time range. Show the current mission-authorized shelter or EVA status alongside logged exposure. |
| Cabin air, thermal load and surface dust | Orion removes CO₂ and moisture with a regenerative amine system. Apollo records document adverse effects of lunar dust introduced into crewed vehicles. NASA standards call for dust control before entering the airlock and for appropriate environmental limits. Lunar dust controls and evidence apply to lunar operations. [Orion life support](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/crew-systems/) · [Apollo lunar dust report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20050160460) · [NASA dust controls](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/9-0-hardware-and-equipment-vol-2/) | Log cabin CO₂, temperature, ventilation state and location alongside symptoms. Show appropriate checklists by mission phase, including post-EVA dust control for surface scenarios. |

## A Mars-transit prototype example

A crew member reports several days of poor sleep, heavier workload and feeling isolated. They can review the changes against their own history, select the information to share and request a private conversation. The designated onboard support path is available during a link outage. Once Earth contact is restored, a selected message can be sent and its receipt tracked. With a one-way delay of 22 minutes, even an immediate reply from Earth would take at least 44 minutes to reach the crew after transmission. The crew can record an agreed schedule adjustment and review symptoms on the next check-in. For a medical event, the CMO can access the local mission procedure, inventory and prior measurements, and transmit a case summary when a link becomes available. These are proposed workflows based on NASA communications and medical-operations concepts. [NASA private communication](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/) · [Earth Independent Medical Operations concept](https://doi.org/10.1177/10806032241310386)

Use synthetic crew check-ins in the public demo. Label environmental replay by detector and mission. The [in-flight inventory](astronaut-inflight-data.md#other-verified-downloads-and-access-limits) links to the Orion Artemis I radiation and Artemis II temperature and humidity examples. NASA describes request-based access for processed, anonymized Artemis II research data. [NASA Artemis II postflight research](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-2/nasas-artemis-ii-moon-mission-research-continues-on-earth/)
