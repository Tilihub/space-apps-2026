# In-flight astronaut data and NASA monitoring priorities

Primary-source inventory checked 2026-11-14. It covers the health domains in NASA's Spaceflight Standard Measures (SSM), the core vital signs, and environmental measurements that help interpret exposure. Public tables provide the reported values and cadence. Several studies provide individual records through a formal access request. The [original research notes](astronaut-health-research.md) cover measurement methods, radiation physics, additional datasets, and the RadLab requests.

## What NASA monitors

**Periodic health checks in flight** cover temperature, blood pressure, pulse, oxygen saturation, and respiratory rate, alongside a physical evaluation. This list comes from the Medical Operations measures in Table 1 of NASA's SSM paper. NASA's astronaut medical standard requires periodic evaluations under [6002]. The requirement describes historically collected vital signs and calls for testing based on symptoms. Orion carries vital-sign, ECG, and oximetry devices for in-mission medical care. [SSM paper, Table 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-025-00532-6.pdf) · [OCHMO-STD-100.1A, §6.1.2](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/ochmo-std-100.1a.pdf) · [Orion medical equipment](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/crew-systems/)

**Mission health and performance** depend on the crew, destination, duration, exposure profile, and ability to return to Earth. NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1 [V1 3003–3004] ties medical capability to these risks. NASA's SSM program monitors sleep and circadian rhythms, fatigue and stress, cognitive performance, teamwork, immune function and viral reactivation, nutrition and blood chemistry, the microbiome, cardiovascular and sensorimotor function, and muscular strength. Medical Operations adds personal radiation dosimetry, eye exams, body mass, aerobic fitness and exercise monitoring, and pre/post bone densitometry. The tables identify domains and collection phases without ranking indicators across missions. [NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1, §3](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/3-0-health-and-medical-care/) · [SSM paper, Tables 1–2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-025-00532-6.pdf)

**Spacecraft and spacesuit safety** has explicit engineering requirements. NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2 [V2 6020–6022] calls for continuous recording, display, and alerts for cabin pressure, humidity, temperature, and oxygen and carbon dioxide partial pressures. Spacesuit requirements [V2 11036–11038] cover suit pressure and gas alerts and the measurement or calculation and display of suited metabolic rate. The astronaut medical standard [6022] separately requires personal radiation dosimetry. [Volume 2, §6](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-natural-and-induced-environments-vol-2/) · [Volume 2, §11](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/11-0-spacesuits-vol-2/) · [OCHMO-STD-100.1A, §6](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/ochmo-std-100.1a.pdf)

## Public results from real crews during spaceflight

The [prototype CSV](astronaut-inflight-vitals.csv) transcribes the five vital signs from published in-flight results. Units appear in the column names (bpm, breaths/min, percent, mmHg, °C). Blank cells indicate an unavailable value. Rows correspond to source observations or summaries, with the mission phase, observation type, context, source URL and table or section locator. A cohort mean represents the population and condition stated in its source. Mission time is source-relative and can be blank for pooled results.

| Indicator | Primary data or public results | Granularity and access |
|---|---|---|
| Heart rate and respiratory rate | NASA's Apollo–Soyuz medical report, Table 3-I, gives average and peak heart rate (beats/min) and respiratory rate (breaths/min) for individual crew roles during exercise on selected flight days, as well as launch and entry. [NASA report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19770023791/downloads/19770023791.pdf) | Public numeric PDF table. Transcribe by role, phase, measurement, and aggregation. Preserve poor or unavailable measurements as gaps. |
| Heart rate and exertion in a spacesuit | Apollo 17 Table 11-I lists average heart rate and reported metabolic rate for individual roles during lunar and transearth EVAs. Figures show parts of the heart-rate time series. [NASA mission report, §11](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a17/A17_MissionReport.pdf) | Public PDF table and figures. Preserve the activity window. The report estimated metabolic rate by different methods depending on the activity and available inputs. |
| Blood pressure, pulse, cardiac output and venous flow | An 11-person ISS ultrasound study has a public table with systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure (mmHg), HR, stroke volume and cardiac output at about flight days 50 and 150, with and without lower body negative pressure. Figures show internal jugular vein cross-sectional area, pressure and flow grades. Skylab 4 reports resting and stressed HR and blood pressure under its own protocol. [ISS cardiovascular table and figures](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2755307) · [NASA Skylab report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19750006309/downloads/19750006309.pdf) | ISS table is group means and 95% confidence intervals. Skylab has crew-role summaries and plots. Keep posture, test conditions and mission phase with each value. |
| Wearable HR, HRV, SpO₂ and energy expenditure | Inspiration4 Apple Watch Series 6 data are plotted in Extended Data Fig. 7. The crew wore the watches on flight days 2–3. In-flight SpO₂ measurements failed for two of four crew members. An ISS Bio-Monitor case report also describes ECG, breathing and oximetry measurements. [Inspiration4 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11357997/) · [ISS Bio-Monitor case study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41360141/) | Public figures and published results. Public downloads for point-by-point crew values and original continuous ECG/PPG waveforms were unverified. |
| Core temperature | ISS study reports estimated core temperature at rest and with exercise, using a forehead heat flux sensor. Figures and supplements document mission-stage changes. [Stahn et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-15560-w) | Published observations by test condition. Public continuous data files were unverified. Label this measure as estimated core temperature and preserve exercise/rest context. |
| Sleep, circadian timing and activity | A Shuttle/ISS actigraphy study reports sleep duration and efficiency by flight phase. A Mir study publishes sleep-stage plots and Supplementary Table S1 with Nightcap sleep outcomes. [Shuttle and ISS actigraphy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4188436/) · [Mir sleep study and supplements](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.14345) | Published summary tables or plots. The Mir authors direct requests for underlying de-identified recordings to NASA's archive. The authors list the DOCX supplement, whose download was unverified. |
| Cognition, alertness, mood and teamwork | In a 25-astronaut ISS study, Table 2 gives group summaries of speed, accuracy and other task measures by mission phase. Table 1 and its Figshare copy contain preflight baseline summaries. NASA's SSM paper describes in-flight sleep and team surveys. [ISS cognition study, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11614644/#T2) · [SSM paper, Table 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-025-00532-6.pdf) | Public group cognition results. Underlying ISS records are archived in LSDA. No open per-astronaut stream for mood or team responses was verified. |
| Ocular health and sensorimotor performance | ISS SANS study reports in-flight OCT measurements, including change in total retinal thickness for eyes with repeated observations. Inspiration4 reports ocular alignment and sensorimotor findings. [ISS SANS study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/2839508) · [Inspiration4 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11357997/) | Published figures, cohort results and study methods. Downloadable raw OCT scans and complete crew test time series were unverified. |
| Hematology and nutritional biochemistry | In a study of 14 ISS astronauts, breath CO elimination and iron-related markers were measured in flight. Supplementary Tables 1–5 list study-level results including iron, ferritin, transferrin saturation and hemolysis markers. A 52-astronaut study reports blood metabolites through flight and recovery, with Supplementary Data 1 giving annotated metabolite results. [ISS hemolysis study and workbook listing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8799460/#Sec12) · [ISS metabolomics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13473307/) | Public published results. Hemolysis XLSX binary delivery was unverified and individual source data require a request. Raw spectra from selected samples also have a public research deposit, with specialist processing required. [Zenodo spectra](https://zenodo.org/records/20591730) |
| Glucose, electrolytes, renal chemistry and hormones | The Skylab plasma biochemistry chapter lists in-flight sodium, potassium, chloride, creatinine, glucose, osmolality, calcium and phosphate (Table V), plus endocrine measures including cortisol and insulin (Table VI). NASA also reports urine chemistry and fluid intake data from Shuttle flights in Table 2-3 of its Extended Duration Orbiter report. [NASA Skylab biomedical report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19750006309/downloads/19750006309.pdf) · [NASA Extended Duration Orbiter medical report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20040201524/downloads/20040201524.pdf) | Public PDF summaries of periodic samples. Skylab reports means and standard errors with sample counts and mission days. The analyte units are printed with each table. |
| Microbiome and immune biology | The Inspiration4 OSD-572 MetaPhlAn table contains processed taxonomic abundance columns from skin, oral and nasal swabs before, during and after flight. A direct TSV download returned 2,723 data rows and 330 columns, with flight-day sample labels such as `C001_FD2_ARM`. Related studies describe host immune profiles and crew/capsule microbiome sampling. [Verified OSD-572 TSV](https://osdr.nasa.gov/geode-py/ws/studies/OSD-572/download?source=datamanager&file=GLDS-564_GMetagenomics_Metaphlan-taxonomy_GLmetagenomics.tsv) · [Primary microbiome study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-024-01635-8) | A comment line precedes the `clade_name` header. Join sample IDs to study metadata for phase/site. These are research markers. Raw human sequence access depends on the study. |
| Viral reactivation | NASA's SSM tracks herpesvirus in saliva. A published ISS case study reports saliva PCR, immune and endocrine measures during a long mission. [SSM paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-025-00532-6.pdf) · [ISS case study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9028032/) | Published case results. Generalization and population baseline estimates require a larger cohort. |
| Body mass, nutrition, hydration, exercise and muscle/bone adaptation | NASA documents flown Skylab measurements of body mass, dietary and excreta mineral balance, urine specific gravity, oxygen uptake and exercise cardiovascular responses. A modern ISS exercise study includes onboard exercise records and pre/post outcomes such as muscle strength and bone density. [NASA Skylab integrated analysis, Table 3-10](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002194/downloads/20200002194.pdf?attachment=true) · [ISS exercise countermeasures study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9898566/) | Skylab report documents provenance and historical measurements. Modern individual exercise records are request-based. SSM lists DXA bone-density measurements as pre/post and some exercise and muscle tests as pre/in/post. |
| Personal radiation exposure | NASA's Apollo radiation report Table I gives mission-average absorbed skin doses from crew-worn passive dosimeters. NASA's Apollo–Soyuz report gives crew dosimetry summaries. [Apollo personal dosimetry](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/tnD7080RadProtect.pdf) · [Apollo–Soyuz, §9.2.6](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19760023154/downloads/19760023154.pdf?attachment=true) | Public PDF summaries. Retain the original quantity and unit (rad). An absorbed skin dose and an effective dose characterize different quantities. |

For the demo, Apollo–Soyuz and the ISS cardiovascular table supply real in-flight vital-sign snapshots. The OSDR and PDS files supply machine-readable flight measurements. Physiological replay with per-second sampling is available from the terrestrial BIDMC data. Treat paper means as cohort observations and keep their phase, units, and aggregation alongside the values. Use NASA's listed clinical vital signs for field selection and keep the limitation of public individual time-series data visible.
